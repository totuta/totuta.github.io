Just before the COVID-19 pandemic, as BTS's popularity rose unmistakably to the level of global stardom, one interpretation went something like this:

- BTS's androgynous, or perhaps gay, appeal resonated with LGBTQ audiences, and that was one of the important reasons for their popularity.

To understand exactly where this interpretation came from, we would have to look at its broader context. Still, it is hard to deny that it captures one facet of the truth.

What is interesting is that the Beatles, who are often compared with BTS, were also interpreted in similar terms at the time, hard as that may be to imagine today.

Compared with other male stars of their era, the Beatles presented a distinctly different kind of masculinity. They were not macho, nor did they make a point of displaying their masculinity. You can see the difference immediately by comparing them with Elvis Presley, an idol to musicians of the time, the Beatles included. What is more, Brian Epstein, the manager who was instrumental in the Beatles' success, was himself gay. Fans also know that he substantially changed the Beatles' image strategy, shaping it into the form we recognize today.
